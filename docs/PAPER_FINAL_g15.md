## 3. The figure finding, into the paper and beside `AGE-01`

**Into the paper.** `papers/draft.md` §3.2 gains a methods paragraph after
*inherited obstructions are re-tested, not carried*, headed *a figure is labelled
from the code; prose need not be*. It states the general form — a sentence can
carry a scope label nobody checks, an axis cannot be drawn until someone
decides what it is — and gives `COR-2` as the worked instance: three sentences
placed the observation-set measurement in chart L, the script that draws
Figure 2 had to read the chart from the producing code and found chart G at
`d=16`, the numbers were right at every site and only the label was wrong, and
it had passed a guard that checks a label is present and cannot check it is
right. The paragraph ends with the conclusion the ruling asked for, that a figure
is a stronger correctness check on a claim than prose is, and with the practice
that follows from it — each result figure is generated by a script that reads
its labels from the artefact or the code, not from the text.

**Beside `AGE-01`.** `docs/RULES_ENACTED.md` gains `FILL-01` — *a presence check
manufactures the value that satisfies it* — as a `###` sub-entry under `AGE-01`
rather than as a `##` section. The reason is structural and is stated in the
entry: `tests/test_rules_enacted_g8.py` reads every `##` section as an enacted
rule and requires it to name a guard that exists with both controls, which is
`OPS-01`. The ruling orders that no guard be built for this class, and the
entry says why — a guard that checked a label is *correct* would need the code
that decides correctness, which is what a figure already reads, so a prose-side
checker would be a presence check on the presence check. A `##` section with no
guard would fail the suite and would also be a lie about what the entry is. The
sub-entry carries the instance, the class, the reason there is no guard, and the
one other check in the tree with the same shape (`SKIP-01`), named so that if it
ever manufactures a value the record already says where to look.

## 5. The two antecedents

**Natterer 1977, cited early.** §3.2's chart paragraph now opens with it: the
premise that the discretisation parameter *is* the regularisation parameter for
projection methods is stated as Natterer's, as old, and as the reason a
parameterisation and its dimension are part of an identifiable count rather
than a presentational choice. The related-work engagement in §2.2(d) is
unchanged and still says what the paper adds on that axis — the measurement of
how far each of interpolant and dimension moves the spectrum, which the
projection-regularisation literature establishes in principle and does not
quantify for this problem.

**Wieland et al. 2021, stated as a limitation and answered.** §2.2(c)'s second
paragraph no longer stops at the concession. It now says the two things that
follow: the rank results of §4.1 and §4.2 inherit the Fisher-information
criticism, reported in the cautious form that family admits — `rank(cutoff)`, a
curve with its cutoff and population gap stated, never a bare integer — but a
cautious Fisher-information claim is still one; and **§4.3 is not subject to
it**, because the barrier measurements, the ridge-against-basin classification
and the witness geometry rest on a profile-likelihood construction, the
instrument the review argues for. §5 gains the matching limitation bullet, so a
reader who goes to the limitations list finds the criticism there with its
scope, not only in related work.
